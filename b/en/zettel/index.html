IOperation

From occurrence to time: operations become a history only in their connection.

  • 1The BridgeIt is not the world that grounds the connection – the connection invents the world that afterwards makes it appear necessary.
  • 2The EventWhat has happened has happened; its history changes with every connection.
  • 3The ConnectionContinuity is not a state – it is the most improbable of all operations.
  • 4The ObserverThe observer can observe the distinction of their present observing only later.
  • 5The Blind SpotThe blind spot is not the end of knowledge – it is its beginning.
  • 6The DelayThe present is the art of overlooking its own delay.
  • 7TimeTime is the condition that something other than its repetition can follow an operation.

IIRecursion

How the past remains effective in traces, practices and narratives.

  • 8The StoreStorage keeps something available; resumption gives it a new present.
  • 9MemoryMemory makes the past effective again in changed practices.
  • 10RememberingRemembering gives what has happened a present, verifiable shape.
  • 11The TraceThe trace holds a past effect materially without fixing its meaning.
  • 12ForgettingForgetting changes what remains within reach, and for whom.
  • 13The ArchiveThe archive keeps and orders what can be found again in the future.
  • 14HistoryHistory interprets the past anew and remains bound to its traces.

IIIAddress

How recognisability is socially attributed and becomes expectable.

  • 15The AddressThe address makes social attribution possible across absence.
  • 16The NameThe name makes absence addressable.
  • 17The PersonThe person is the memory of an address.
  • 18The RoleThe role makes expectations independent of persons.
  • 19ExpectationExpectations order what can come as a surprise at an address.
  • 20ResponsibilityResponsibility binds consequences to an address.
  • 21RecognitionRecognition gives an address social validity.

IVCoupling

How separate orders become effective for one another without uniting.

  • 22The BoundaryOnly what remains separate can touch.
  • 23TouchTouch is the boundary in the form of an event.
  • 24IrritationIrritation is an outside effect in a form of one's own.
  • 25The BodyThe body turns outside effects into sensitivity.
  • 26PerceptionPerception makes the boundary between body and consciousness questionable.
  • 27TranslationTranslation is repetition without identity.
  • 28CouplingCoupling stabilises effects at boundaries whose emergence itself remains questionable.

VArt

How differences of perception remain socially reusable.

  • 29The MaterialMaterial is the resistance through which form becomes possible.
  • 30The FormForm makes difference reusable.
  • 31The WorkA work remains recognisable as a particular form; an artistic address can carry several works.
  • 32StyleStyle is the recurrence of a difference as expectation.
  • 33The AudienceThe audience cultivates the sensitivity of separate perceptions.
  • 34The ExhibitionThe exhibition orders relations before it orders meanings.
  • 35ArtArt keeps perception socially reusable by keeping its difference open.

VIPlatform

How recognisability is turned into probability – and probability is returned again as environment.

  • 36The ProfileThe profile turns addressable traces into calculable recognisability.
  • 37InformationInformation arises when a difference has consequences within a particular order.
  • 38ProbabilityProbability turns open contingency into an unequally weighted future.
  • 39The AlgorithmAlgorithmic ranking distributes unequal chances of the next connection.
  • 40The RecommendationThe recommendation returns calculated probability as a possible next world.
  • 41The FeedThe feed makes prediction the present environment of its next verification.
  • 42The PlatformThe platform returns calculated possibilities as environment and processes their consequences.

VIIFuture

Under what conditions, despite these reductions, going on otherwise remains possible.

  • 43IdentityIdentity makes change attributable across time.
  • 44PossibilityPossibility is the present reachability of a connection not yet actualised.
  • 45ContingencyContingency becomes a chance for the future where alternatives remain within reach and structures remain revisable.
  • 46The InterruptionAn interruption opens up the future only if something can connect differently after it.
  • 47The NewNew is what changes what is possible afterwards.
  • 48FreedomFreedom is the ability to take up, reject or reshape structured contingency.
  • 49The FutureThe future is the preserved room to go on otherwise than is already expected.
