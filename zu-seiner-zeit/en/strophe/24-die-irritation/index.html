Irritation

Irritation is an outside effect in a form of one's own.

An irritation does not penetrate a system from outside. It arises when a foreign change triggers consequences within the system's own distinctions. The same sound can startle, soothe or go unnoticed; it is not the stimulus that determines the reaction but the order that processes it as a deviation. Irritation therefore proves neither openness nor closure alone. It designates the paradoxical point at which a system depends on something it cannot take over. The foreign becomes effective by losing its foreignness and being continued as a difference of the system's own. Coupling begins where such irritations become expectable again.
