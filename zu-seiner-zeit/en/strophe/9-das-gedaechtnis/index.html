Memory

Memory makes the past effective again in changed practices.

Memory shows itself when the past becomes effective again in a present practice. A story calls up earlier events differently from a gesture, a practised movement of the hand or a ritual. Such resumptions have a history; they take place under conditions that have changed. The body, too, carries habits and abilities without having an image of the past ready at every moment. Memory can therefore be reduced neither to filing nor to conscious recollection. It is bound to materials, bodies and social forms and remains at the same time a present achievement. What returns, returns in a new situation.
