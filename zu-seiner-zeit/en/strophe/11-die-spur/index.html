The Trace

The trace holds a past effect materially without fixing its meaning.

A footprint in the sand, a yellowed photograph or a crease in paper remembers nothing. Yet every trace has a material constitution that makes later interpretations possible and limits them. The print does not by itself say who passed by; nor can any movement whatsoever be attributed to it. Whether a trace becomes evidence, a story, a work of art or rubbish is decided in a later practice. Its material remains stubborn in the process. The trace links a past occurrence with possible present questions without already carrying the answer within itself.
