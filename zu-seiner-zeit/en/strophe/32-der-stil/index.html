Style

Style is the recurrence of a difference as expectation.

Style is not the handwriting in which an unmistakable interior betrays itself. The work binds form to an address; style arises when, at this address, particular ways of choosing, linking and leaving out become recognisable across different works. A rhythm, a blank, a tension of colour or even a refusal can recur without repeating itself. That is why style can change media, outlive the artist and detach itself from them – as a school, an epoch or a manner. Precisely therein lies its danger: what first produced difference congeals into expectation and finally demands of the new that it look once more like its earlier deviation.
