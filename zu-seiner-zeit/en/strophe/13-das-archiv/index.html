The Archive

The archive keeps and orders what can be found again in the future.

An archive keeps documents and objects. At the same time it orders them according to rules that determine what can be taken in, described and found again. These rules create future possibilities and limit them: without cataloguing, even what has been preserved remains hard to reach; what was never collected cannot simply be called back. A file becomes evidence only through questions, procedures and contradiction. Archives therefore do not tell history by themselves. They provide material and shape which histories can be made from it. Their achievement lies in combining preservation, selection and access – and thus also in the power to set these conditions.
