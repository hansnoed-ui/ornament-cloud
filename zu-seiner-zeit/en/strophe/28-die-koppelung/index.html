Coupling

Coupling stabilises effects at boundaries whose emergence itself remains to be questioned.

Coupling designates a relationship in which distinguishable processes repeatedly become effective for one another. A body does not bring organism, nervous system, consciousness and communication together into a single operation. At it, transitions arise at which each side forms events of its own from effects. Repetition makes such irritations expectable without abolishing differences. The concept thereby explains how separate orders can depend on one another. A further question remains open: how are the boundaries drawn at which we distinguish these orders in the first place? It cannot be answered by appealing to coupling once more, and it makes the relationship itself the object of observation.
