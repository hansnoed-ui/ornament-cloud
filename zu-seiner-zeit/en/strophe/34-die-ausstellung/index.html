The Exhibition

The exhibition orders relations before it orders meanings.

An exhibition does not simply show works. It sets them in relation. Closeness, distance, sequence, light, architecture, labelling and routing help determine what becomes visible at the same time and what conceals what. The same work can ask a different question in a different neighbourhood. The exhibition therefore produces no shared perception, but it distributes possibilities of seeing: it accelerates, delays, isolates, condenses and lets transitions emerge. Its order remains provisional; with every step the relation between things changes again. An exhibition is therefore less a container than a choreography – a spatial form of coupling in which works, bodies and expectations gain new sensitivities for one another.
