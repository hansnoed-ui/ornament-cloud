VII · Future · 47 / 49

The New

New is what changes what is possible afterwards.

The new is not simply what was not there before. Surprises can remain without consequence, fashions disappear, coincidences fit at once into familiar patterns. Novelty becomes theoretically interesting only where an event changes what is possible afterwards. It shifts expectations, rules, perceptions or relationships and produces forms of connection that were not reachable in the same way before. That is why repetition, too, can be new: when it changes its conditions. The new does not stand outside history but intervenes in its continuation. It is not a property of an object but a difference in the space of the possibilities that follow.
