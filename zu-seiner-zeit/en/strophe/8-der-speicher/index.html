The Store

Storage keeps something available; resumption gives it a new present.

A library keeps books, a hard drive data. Such stores keep material states available and protect them against rapid disappearance. That does not yet explain why something gains meaning years later. A recovered book has to be read, a data set selected, a find brought into a question. Storage creates a possible later encounter; remembering begins with its present performance. The two often need each other, but they do not coincide. Even a fully preserved document can become illegible if its language and its context are lost. The store secures availability; it does not decide the next use.
