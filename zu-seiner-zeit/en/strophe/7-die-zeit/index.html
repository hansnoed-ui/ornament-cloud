Time

Time is the condition that something other than its repetition can follow an operation.

Time does not carry events like a current from one place to another. An operation ends with its performance; what has happened cannot be repeated as if it were still present. At the same time a later operation can connect to it and, under changed conditions, make something else of it. Here lies the tension between past and future: what has happened is fixed, its possible continuations are not. Duration arises when connections become possible again; a break changes their conditions. In this stanza, time names the difference that every resumption carries with it: what comes next can connect without going on in the same way.
