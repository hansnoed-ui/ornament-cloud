History

History interprets the past anew and remains bound to its traces.

Past events have happened, even if no one tells of them any longer. History arises when a society orders, disputes and reinterprets its traces, testimonies and traditions. With every present, other questions and previously overlooked documents can become important. That changes the history that can be told; what has happened does not thereby become arbitrary. A claim must prove itself against the available traces, against other accounts and against their gaps. History is neither identical with the archive nor the mere sum of memories. It is a contested, present work on a past that is not exhausted by its use today.
