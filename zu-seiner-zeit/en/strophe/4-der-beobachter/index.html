The Observer

The observer can observe the distinction of their present observing only later.

To observe means to use a distinction and to indicate one of its sides. The observer does not stand over against this operation as an additional authority: the observer becomes determinable only through the way of observing. While seeing something, the observer cannot at the same time observe, as the same operation, the distinction with which they are seeing. A later observation can make this precondition visible; to do so it again uses a distinction of its own. Self-observation thus shifts its blind spot instead of abolishing it once and for all. Even the supposed beginning of observing appears as a beginning only from a later perspective.
