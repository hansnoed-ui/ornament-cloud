Touch

Touch is the boundary in the form of an event.

Touch does not abolish a boundary. It is the moment in which two separate orders produce consequences in each other. One hand does not feel the other as the other feels itself; each side produces its own event from the contact. Precisely for this reason touch can be neither possession nor fusion. It changes without transferring anything unchanged. In it the boundary becomes sensitive: pressure, warmth, resistance and closeness appear only because bodies do not become one another. Social touch, too, follows this logic. It lets the foreign become effective without turning it into one's own. Where touch succeeds, difference is preserved not despite closeness but through it.
