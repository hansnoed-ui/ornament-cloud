Forgetting

Forgetting changes what remains within reach, and for whom.

Forgetting takes several forms. A document can be destroyed, an ability can wither, a name can disappear from public records. In other cases a trace remains, but the paths to it become unusable or improbable. Social forgetting thus helps to order what can be told, examined or expected again. This selection brings relief; but it can also push people and events out of reachable memory. Forgetting is therefore neither merely a lack nor always a creative achievement. Its consequences depend on what is lost, for whom something becomes inaccessible and who can decide about it.
