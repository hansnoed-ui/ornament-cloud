The Person

The person is the memory of an address.

A person is not the human being it designates. It is the form in which communication bundles numerous expectations onto a recognisable address. Whoever is considered friendly, unreliable, ill, responsible or dangerous is not fully described but prepared for further connections. Therein lies the relief the person provides: no one has to be invented anew in every encounter. Therein lies at the same time its harshness: what was once attributed returns as expectation and limits what can still appear credible in future. Persons therefore consist neither of properties nor of inwardness. They come into being as addressings condense and expect their own repetition.
