Translation

Translation is repetition without identity.

Translation transfers nothing unchanged from one order into another. Perception does not become language, pain does not become a message, and a thought does not become communication. Each side produces a form of its own from what reaches it. Therein lies the loss in every translation – and at the same time its possibility: only because sameness is unattainable can something be continued under changed conditions. Here translation touches on Deleuze's concept of repetition, which reproduces nothing identical but brings forth difference. Unlike in Deleuze, however, the concern here is not primarily the creative unfolding of the new, but connection between operationally separate orders. Translation does not remove difference; it makes it connectable.
