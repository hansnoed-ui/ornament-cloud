The Address

The address makes social attribution possible across absence.

Communication needs addresses to which statements, expectations and answers can be attributed. A name, an organisation or a technical account makes someone or something reachable even when its bearer is absent. Such addresses come into being and change through repeated reference. They are not, however, the origin of every bodily or mental occurrence. Their domain is social attribution: who is addressed as the same place, who can answer, who remains responsible? An address keeps these possibilities open across individual situations. How someone is addressed influences their social identity without coinciding with their life.
