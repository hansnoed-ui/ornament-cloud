The Name

The name makes absence addressable.

A name does not say who someone is. It ensures that the same address can be called up again even when its bearer has become absent, changed or contradictory. Names condense past addressings into a form to which expectations, stories and responsibilities connect. Therein lie their power and their violence. Whoever receives a name becomes socially findable, but also fixed; whoever is renamed loses not just a word but an already organised future of possible attributions. The name therefore belongs entirely to no one. It circulates between one's own use and the use of others and holds a social unity together where no identity is sufficient unto itself.
