Recognition

Recognition gives an address social validity.

Recognition does not discover an identity that already exists. It confirms an address as a valid place of further communication. Whoever is recognised may speak, act, remember and make claims without having to prove their belonging anew in every encounter. Yet recognition is never complete. It grasps a person only in forms that are socially legible, and precisely thereby can overlook or exclude other things about them. Misrecognition, too, keeps addresses effective by attributing alien properties to them. Recognition is therefore not a possession but an ongoing social performance in which it is decided who can appear as whom.
