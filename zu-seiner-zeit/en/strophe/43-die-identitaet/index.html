Identity

Identity makes change attributable across time.

Identity is not the sameness of a thing with itself. It arises where change can be attributed to the same address across time. A person, a work or an organisation does not remain identical because everything stays the same, but because differences are treated as changes of something recognisable. Identity therefore mediates between experience and expectation. It holds on to enough continuity for deviation to be noticed at all, and enough openness for change to remain attributable. Against platform profiles this distinction becomes important: calculable recognisability is not yet identity. Identity is the form in which change can be continued without dissolving into sameness.

    Mirrors and Masks

    Identity appears not as an unchangeable core but as something continually formed in social relationships and biographical transitions.

  2. Philosophy

    Paul Ricœur

    Oneself as Another

    The distinction between idem and ipse separates sameness from selfhood: someone can remain the same without remaining alike in all their properties.

  3. Art

    Gillian Wearing

    Album

    Masked reconstructions of family photographs separate visible resemblance and personal continuity; here the question of one's own identity comes to the fore.

  4. Literature

    Virginia Woolf

    Orlando

    Orlando remains attributable as a figure although centuries, social roles and gender change. Identity becomes narratable through change.
