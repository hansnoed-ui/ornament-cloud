The Event

What has happened has happened; its history changes with every connection.

An event takes place and is over the next moment. What has happened cannot afterwards be undone. What remains open, by contrast, is the place it will be given in a history. A later connection can turn it into a beginning, a mistake, a coincidence or a turning point. The idea of a straight line of development reads a direction into earlier moments that was still open at the time. Every present selects traces and orders them anew. In doing so it changes the history that can be told about what happened. Precisely because the event is past, its meaning is never settled once and for all.
