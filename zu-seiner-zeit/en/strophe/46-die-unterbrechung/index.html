The Interruption

An interruption opens up the future only if something can connect differently after it.

An interruption does not yet produce a future. A failure, a shock or a refusal can just as well destroy a sequence, paralyse it or force it back into its old form. What matters is whether the interruption makes a different continuation connectable. Only then does it change the structure of going on. This holds for conversations, institutions, works of art and technical systems. Interruption is therefore not a romantic counter-figure to order. It is a test of order: can it take in deviation without immediately normalising it or breaking off? The future does not arise in the rupture itself, but in the connections the rupture makes possible.

Thinking further

  1. Sociology

    Harold Garfinkel

    Studies in Ethnomethodology / Breaching Experiments

    Disruptions of everyday expectations often provoke work of repair. A breach of norms does not automatically produce a new order.

  2. Philosophy

    Hannah Arendt

    The Human Condition

    With natality, Arendt links action with the possibility of a beginning. What matters is not the break alone but that a new sequence can begin.

  3. Art

    Doris Salcedo

    Shibboleth

    A crack in the floor of Tate Modern makes separation spatially present and forces the institution to perceive architecture and history differently.

  4. Literature

    Samuel Beckett

    Waiting for Godot

    Countless interruptions are followed by no real new beginning. The negative case shows that deferral does not necessarily change the structure of continuation.
