Remembering

Remembering gives what has happened a present, verifiable shape.

Remembering happens in the present. When an experience is told, an image looked at or an earlier sentence read again, the past takes on a present-day shape. This shape can change without the past event itself becoming a different one after the fact. Memories draw on traces, fill in gaps and are bound to expectations. They can contradict one another; objects and testimonies at the same time set limits to their interpretations. What is remembered is therefore neither an unchanged copy nor a free invention. Remembering establishes a relation to what has happened and can only be questioned anew from later presents.
