The Audience

The audience cultivates the sensitivity of separate perceptions.

An audience is not a crowd that sees the same thing. Perception remains untransferable; no one can take over another person's colour, tension or boredom. Commonality therefore arises not in experience but in what connects to it. People compare, tell, contradict, come back, and in doing so learn to become sensitive to other things. The audience does not unite perceptions. It cultivates conditions under which separate perceptions can irritate and change one another. Therein lies its peculiar sociality: one does not have to have seen the same thing in order to go on seeing the same work differently. The public begins where difference is not removed but reused as a sensitivity that can change.
