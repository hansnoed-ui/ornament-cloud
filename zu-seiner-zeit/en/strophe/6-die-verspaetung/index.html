The Delay

The present is the art of overlooking its own delay.

Every present appears to itself as immediate. In fact it always arrives too late. Even before an operation experiences itself as present, it has already passed and can only be taken up again through further operations. What we call presence is therefore not a point in time but an ongoing reconstruction. The present lives on continually forgetting its own delay. It is precisely this small lag that makes memory, expectation and connection possible. Without it there would be neither time nor history, only the silent extinguishing of isolated events.
