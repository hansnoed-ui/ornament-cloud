Responsibility

Responsibility binds consequences to an address.

Responsibility does not arise inside a person. It arises when communication attributes an action and its consequences to an address. Whoever is held responsible remains reachable for something that has already happened and yet demands further answers. In this, responsibility links past and future: it holds an address fast across changing situations and expects justification, reparation or change from it. This bond can make action possible, but it can also distribute guilt, shift competences and conceal power. Responsibility therefore does not designate a moral property. It is the social procedure through which consequences receive a place capable of answering.
