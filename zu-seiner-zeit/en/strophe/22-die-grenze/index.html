The Boundary

Only what remains separate can touch.

A boundary does not lie between systems that are already complete. It comes into being with every operation that continues something and excludes something else. Only this distinction produces an inside to which further operations can be attributed, and an outside that does not disappear but remains effective as environment. Boundaries therefore do not prevent relationship; they make it possible. Only what does not dissolve into one another can touch, irritate or couple. The boundary is not a wall but a continually renewed form at which one's own and the other come into being at the same time. Where it falls, what begins is not unity but the loss of any distinguishable relationship.
