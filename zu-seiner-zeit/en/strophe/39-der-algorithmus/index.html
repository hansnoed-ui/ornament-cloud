The Algorithm

Algorithmic ranking distributes unequal chances of the next connection.

On platforms, algorithmic selection procedures order content and suggestions: more or less relevant, visible or invisible, first or later. Such procedures can calculate probabilities from data and translate them into a concrete ranking. This requires prior decisions about what is collected, labelled and counted as success. The power of this ranking lies in preparing the present unequally. What ranks higher is more likely to get the chance to trigger a further connection; what is rarely shown is more easily overlooked. Users can object or choose differently. But their deviations, too, meet an already ordered selection and can enter again as data.
