The Body

The body turns outside effects into sensitivity.

The body is not a bridge across which the world reaches consciousness unchanged. It is a membrane that lets nothing become effective without transforming it. Light, pressure, temperature or someone else's gaze are not taken over but translated into sensations, movements, tensions and readinesses. Consciousness, too, does not have the body at its disposal as if it were its possession. It encounters it as pain, pleasure, exhaustion or restlessness – as a closeness that never becomes fully transparent. Communication can name, observe and address these states, but it can take over neither the pain nor the perception of another. In the body, the environment becomes sensitive without ceasing to be environment. It couples by separating, and connects by forcing every effect into a form of its own.
