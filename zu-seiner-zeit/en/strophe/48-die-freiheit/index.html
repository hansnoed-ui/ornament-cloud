Freedom

Freedom is the ability to take up, reject or reshape structured contingency.

Freedom is neither arbitrariness nor the absence of conditions. Action is always structured: by bodies, language, history, institutions, technology and other people. Freedom therefore shows itself not in beginning without preconditions, but in being able to deal with them differently. An expectation can be adopted, rejected, shifted or reshaped; a rule can be followed, interpreted or made the object of new rules. Freedom needs possibilities, but also abilities and conditions to perceive them and work on them. It is a structured disposal over contingency. Unfreedom begins where alternatives formally exist but no longer become practically connectable.
