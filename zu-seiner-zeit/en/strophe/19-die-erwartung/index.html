Expectation

Expectations order what can come as a surprise at an address.

Expectations do not predict with certainty what will happen. They determine what can count as fitting, deviant or surprising. A name, a role and a familiar relationship bring such expectations with them; through them the future is already ordered in the present. Disappointments can change an expectation. But they can also lead to its reinforcement when the deviation is treated as an exception or attributed to a person. Precisely therein lies its social persistence. An expectation links an address with possible next events and at the same time determines how the unexpected can be dealt with.
