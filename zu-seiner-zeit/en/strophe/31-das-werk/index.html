The Work

A work remains recognisable as a particular form; an artistic address can carry several works.

Not every form is a work. A work needs conditions under which, when executed differently or viewed later, it can be recognised as this work: a title, for instance, a shape, a rule of performance or a documented action. This is to be distinguished from the address of an artist, to which several works can be attributed. Bazon Brock's “artist without a work” shifts precisely the relationship between these two addresses. The later knowledge of Duchamp's Étant donnés changes the story about his public pause from making works, without turning his entire output into a single work. The identity of a work arises through the repeatable attribution of a particular form.
